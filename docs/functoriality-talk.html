A talk for category theorists · mechanism design · Lean 4

Functoriality is a correctness condition on economic definitions

The project began as a search for a common language — category theory already describes design in systems engineering; could it reach the socio-institutional kind? Two classical theorems, Myerson's Revenue Equivalence and the Mirrlees Taxation Principle, do turn out to be one theorem about an adjunction. But that is not the interesting claim, and this talk will not make it. The interesting claim is that demanding a functor refuted a definition nobody had ever been asked to justify — not a belief economists hold, but a default they had never had occasion to examine — and that a proof assistant is what made the refutation impossible to talk our way around.

Provenance

Why I went looking for this in the first place

Talks usually present the argument in the order that makes it look inevitable. This one came about the other way round, and the detours are the part worth reporting — because every one of them was a wrong guess that got corrected, and that is the case being made.

I was looking for a common language. Category theory already describes technical elements of design in parts of systems engineering — compositional specification, interfaces, refinement. My interest is the socio-institutional side of design: mechanisms, taxes, institutions. If the same language reaches both, that is worth something well beyond a single theorem, and it seemed at least possible that it did.

So I read around, settled on Categories for the Working Mathematician as the spine, handed it to Claude together with the revenue-equivalence literature, and asked for a curriculum that would let me state Myerson's theorem in categorical language. Nothing more ambitious than that: a translation exercise, to find out whether the vocabulary could even hold the statement.

It could. And in doing so it produced the observation that started the real work: the if-and-only-if — the two-way street of an adjunction — looked a great deal like the Taxation Principle. Both theorems relate who gets what to who pays what. But they relate them differently, and the difference is precisely the shape of an adjunction:

Revenue equivalence says

Unique. Given the allocation rule, who gets the object, all auctions yield the same revenue. Technically, the allocation and the rent at the bottom force the payment rule. There is nothing left to choose.

The taxation principle says

Exists. Given the allocation, who works how much and the consumption levels, a payment schedule implementing it can be found — you never need to observe the type, only the outcome.

The vocabulary, once, so nothing later is a guess

Five symbols carry the whole talk. They are the Lean definitions, not paraphrases of them.

SymbolWhat it isIn the two settings
θ The agent's type — the thing she knows and you do not. Myerson: how much the bidder values the object. Mirrlees: how productive the worker is.
A The outcome space — what there is to hand out. All that is ever used of it is that it is ordered. Mirrlees: income earned, output produced. Myerson: an allocation level, read as the probability of winning. Both are ℝ in the formalisation; [0,1] is a hypothesis carried at the call sites that need it, not a property of the type.
q : ℝ → A The allocation rule: what a type gets. Who wins the auction. How much a worker of productivity θ earns.
t : ℝ → ℝ The transfer rule: what a type pays. Positive means money flows from the agent. The bid you are charged. The tax you owe.
v : A → ℝ → ℝ The value function: v(a, θ) is what outcome a is worth to type θ, before paying. Myerson: θ·a. Mirrlees: θ·h(y) − g(y/θ) — earn income y, monetary value of the effort, y/θ, it took to earn this income.
A mechanism

Any set of rules you commit to in advance that turns what people do into an outcome and a payment. A sealed-bid auction is a mechanism; so is an English auction; so is the tax code together with the forms you file. The agent plays a game, and the game deterimines allocations and payments.

Stated that way the space of mechanisms is hopeless — it is the space of all possible games and payments.

A direct mechanism

The mechanism whose only allowed move is to report a type. You say θ; you receive q θ and pay t θ. So a direct mechanism is the pair ⟨q, t⟩, and nothing else.

The revelation principle says this costs nothing: whatever any mechanism achieves in equilibrium, a direct mechanism exits that achieves the same thing by making actors tell the truth (report their types). So the pair (q, t) is fully general — and incentive compatibility is what buys that generality back: it is the requirement that no type prefers to report to be a different type, which is what makes "being told the truth" an equilibrium rather than a hope.

In Lean: Mechanism A is the pair; ICIRMechanism A v θ_min is the pair carrying proofs of monotonicity, IsIC, and IsIR. Every object in this talk is one of those two.

Uniqueness on one side, existence on the other, over the same pair of structures. That is a universal property if anything is. So I set out to see whether both could be one categorical statement.

And my first guess was wrong. I expected a right adjoint — pricing as the structure-preserving right-hand map, mechanisms sitting terminally over their allocations. The formalisation would not have it. Pricing is the left adjoint: T ⊣ Q, and T(q) is initial in its fibre, not terminal. The reason is individual rationality — rents are bounded below by zero, so the rent-extracting mechanism sits at the bottom of the family and arrows run out of it. Once you see it, it is obvious. I did not see it, and I would have tried to publish the other thing.

That is the first of two corrections: the direction of the adjunction, and then later the definition of the morphisms. Both were guesses that felt right. Both were caught by an obligation I could not argue with. Everything that follows is really just an account of what it is like to work in a language that can answer back, in particular when provided with an LLM translator.

Act I

Two thin categories, and one free parameter

The whole argument turns on noticing which of three structures the economics pins down and which it leaves free. Two are forced. One is a modelling choice — and modelling choices are where errors can live.

The one theorem everything else leans on

The envelope theorem (Milgrom–Segal). Let ⟨q, t⟩ be incentive compatible, and write V(θ) = v(q θ, θ) − t θ for what the agent walks away with. Then V has a right derivative everywhere, equal to

V′(θ) = ∂θv(q θ, θ),    hence    V(θ) = V(θmin) + ∫θminθ ∂θv(q s, s) ds

What it says. Only the direct effect of the type on value survives. The effect running through the allocation — the fact that a higher type is also handed a different q θ — contributes nothing, because the agent had already optimised over what to report. Nothing is assumed about q beyond monotonicity: no smoothness, no continuity. IC does all the work.

Why the derivative is one-sided, and why that is not a technicality. A two-sided derivative would be false for a mechanisms the field cares about a lot. A posted price with reserve p gives V(θ) = max(θ − p, 0) — a kink at p, no derivative there. The right derivative survives the kink, so the identity holds where HasDerivAt would not. Act VII is about what happened when this was checked.

The economist says

An allocation rule q : ℝ → A says what a type gets: the probability a bidder wins, or the income a worker earns. It must be monotone — higher types get weakly more. Whether one rule dominates another is not up for debate: pointwise, q θ ≤ q′ θ for every θ.

A mechanism ⟨q, t⟩ adds a price. An auction: who wins, and who pays what. A tax code: what you earn, and what you owe. It must be incentive compatible (no one wants to lie) and individually rational (the lowest type is willing to show up).

Which mechanism dominates which? The economics seems split on this. For an auction the natural answer is maximum revenue for the seller, for the tax system maximum welfare is the goal, so minimal tax payments for the given allocation are better. The only other answer we could give, would be based on the goal of the planner, but these differ per setting.

The category theorist says

Alloc: objects are monotone q : ℝ → A; there is an arrow q ⟶ q′ iff q ≤ q′ pointwise. A thin category — a preorder. Its arrows are handed to us.

Mech: objects are IC+IR mechanisms. Arrows: to be determined. This is the free parameter, and the only one.

There is an obvious forgetful functor Q : Mech ⥤ Alloc — drop the transfer, keep the allocation. It is a functor for any choice of arrow in Mech that refines the allocation order, so Q exerts no discipline. It constrains nothing.

The discipline has to come from a functor going the other way.

Act II

The functor that must exist: envelope pricing

There is exactly one candidate for a map Alloc → Mech, and the economics — not the mathematics — hands it to us.

The economist says

Given an allocation rule, what must you charge so that telling the truth is optimal? The envelope theorem answers: the agent's information rent must accrue at exactly the rate at which her type raises her value. Anything else, and some type will lie.

t*(θ) = v(q θ, θ) − ∫θminθ ∂θv(q s, s) ds

That is Myerson's payment rule, and it is also Mirrlees's tax schedule. It is not a choice. It is the unique truthful price that concedes nothing to the lowest type.

The category theorist says

So we have a map on objects, T : Alloc → Mech, given to us from outside. We did not design it; we are obliged to accept it.

Now the only question a category theorist ever asks: is it a functor?

And here is the point of the whole talk. T is fixed. The order on Alloc is fixed. So the demand

q ≤ q′ ⟹ T(q) ⟶ T(q′)

is not a demand on T at all. It is a constraint on the one thing we were free to choose — the arrows of Mech. Functoriality has become a test on a definition.

Act III

The test runs. One definition dies.

Two things to have straight before the test runs

An arrow here is not a map

Both categories are thin: between any two objects there is at most one arrow. So an arrow carries no data — it is not a function, not a homomorphism, nothing you can apply to anything. It is a claim, and the claim is this one dominates that one. Composition is transitivity; the identity is reflexivity. A thin category is exactly a preorder.

Which makes a functor between them a monotone map, and functoriality the single implication q ≤ q′ ⟹ T(q) ⪯ T(q′). That is the whole of the obligation, and it is why an economist can be handed the test without learning any category theory: it says pricing preserves the ranking.

Faithful — injective on each hom-set — is free here, and worth nothing: hom-sets have at most one element, so every functor between thin categories is faithful. Full — surjective on each hom-set — is not free. It says that whenever T(q) ⪯ T(q′) holds in Mech, it was already recorded by q ≤ q′ in Alloc. Between preorders, fully faithful means precisely order-embedding: q ≤ q′ ⟺ T(q) ⪯ T(q′), ranking for ranking, nothing added and nothing lost. Act V collects the fact that T is one.

Surplus — the quantity everything is measured in

The surplus (or information rent, or indirect utility) is what the agent walks away with once she has paid:

V(θ) = v(q θ, θ) − t(θ)

It is not a derived convenience — it is the agent's objective, and therefore the field's. IC is a statement about it (no type raises her V by lying). IR is a statement about it (V(θmin) ≥ 0, or she stays home). Welfare is it, summed. Revenue is total value minus it — every pound of rent conceded is a pound the seller did not collect. Design in this field is the business generating and dividing this surplus.

And it costs nothing to work in, because of quasilinearity: money enters utility linearly, so t = v − V. Record (q, V) and the price comes back exactly. Nothing has been forgotten.

Hold on to that. Everything a mechanism designer wants is written in V. We are now about to define dominance without it — and watch what the functor does.

Two candidate orders on Mech. One a simple vector ranking, what you write down instinctively; the other is what the economics actually implies. The first is wrong — and the functor law is why.

Candidate A — order mechanisms by transfer

Where this definition came from

Not from a paper. Not from anyone's considered view. A mechanism is a pair ⟨q, t⟩ — that is the definition, in Myerson and in every textbook since. And when your object is a pair, the order you reach for is the componentwise one: allocates more, charges more.

It is also flattered by a real intuition: a bigger bundle costs more. True — of one agent facing one price schedule. This is a comparison between two mechanisms. The intuition was transplanted, and transplanted intuitions are not checked.

The category theorist says

Whatever its provenance, T.map now owes us t*(q) ≤ t*(q′) from q ≤ q′. Work the obligation out and it reduces to v being convex in the allocation.

It isn't. In the Mirrlees setting v(y,θ) = θ·h(y) − g(y/θ) with h concave and g convex — so v is concave in y, precisely the wrong way. The functor does not exist.

Note the shape of what just happened. The category did not contradict an economist's belief. It audited a definition that had never been asked to say anything — and found it saying something false.

The first candidate order was included in the lean defintions, and then had to be mapped. The resulting, LLM-generated code is shown below.

structure MechHom (m m' : ICIRMechanism A v θ_min) : Type where hAlloc : ∀ θ, m.mech.q θ ≤ m'.mech.q θ hTransf : ∀ θ, m.mech.t θ ≤ m'.mech.t θ -- the natural definition def T : MonotoneAlloc A ⥤ ICIRMechanism A v θ_min where obj r := Tmech r map f := { hAlloc := f.h hTransf := sorry } -- ← not provable. For any v.

The sorry that proofs the value of LEAN in this context. It was read, for a long time, as a proof gap witout consequences as no results explicitely called this field.

Verdict

The sorry was not a proof gap. It was the category refusing an incorrect definition. No amount of tactic engineering was ever going to close it, because the statement is false — and it is false exactly in one of the settings used in the paper. Had we used a single model, auctions, the formalization would never have caught it.

Under the transfer order Myerson is fine: v = θ·q is linear, hence convex. It is Mirrlees that kills it. The universal quantifier over settings — the very thing the unification demands — is what supplied the refutation.

And note how quietly it could have died. A componentwise order on a pair is invisible: it looks like bookkeeping, not a claim. It never appears in a theorem statement, so it is never checked; it merely sits in a structure and silently constrains everything downstream. The only reason it was ever read aloud is that a functor demanded to know what it said.

Candidate B — order mechanisms by surplus

The economist says

Both settings have a numeraire: money enters utility linearly. That is what quasilinearity is, and it means the agent's state is not the price she pays but the rent she keeps:

V(θ) = v(q θ, θ) − t(θ)

So order mechanisms by rent. A mechanism dominates another if it allocates more and concedes more surplus. Nothing is lost: quasilinearity means the price is recoverable, t = v − V.

The category theorist says

Now T.map owes us VT(q) ≤ VT(q′). And the envelope formula collapses the surplus of an envelope mechanism to a bare integral:

VT(q)(θ) = ∫θminθ Dq(s) ds,   Dq(s) = ∂θv(q s, s)

So functoriality reduces to monotonicity of the integrand: q ≤ q′ ⟹ Dq ≤ Dq′ pointwise. And that is single crossing — Spence–Mirrlees — which both settings already assume, for reasons that predate this paper by fifty years.

structure MechHom (m m' : ICIRMechanism A v θ_min) : Type where hAlloc : ∀ θ, m.mech.q θ ≤ m'.mech.q θ hSurp : ∀ θ, θ_min ≤ θ → surplus m θ ≤ surplus m' θ theorem Tmech_surplus_mono (hSC : SingleCrossing v) (h : ∀ θ, r.q θ ≤ r'.q θ) : ∀ θ, θ_min ≤ θ → surplus (Tmech r) θ ≤ surplus (Tmech r') θ -- proved from typeDeriv_mono_of_alloc_le + integral_mono_on. Single crossing ALONE.

The functor exists. T.map is the single-crossing step, and nothing else.

Verdict

Functoriality explaines the surplus order. The reason the right state variable is the rent and not the price is that the rent is what pricing is monotone in. An economist can reach this by taste, I did, eventually. Surplus, or indirect utility, is universially good, increasing it (ceteris paribus) always increases the value of the maximization problem. A category theorist will object immediately, and the objection is correct. From category theory, the direction is more direct: the functor law reaches it by obligation.

Act IV

The limit — falsification, not derivation

So what pins the surplus order? Functoriality plus one further requirement, which is economic, not categorical: the order must lose no information. Quasilinearity supplies exactly that. Since t θ = v(q θ, θ) − V(θ), an order recording (q, V) determines the transfer completely, whereas the discrete order records nothing.

The surplus order is therefore the coarsest order on Mech that (a) survives the functor law and (b) forgets nothing. Two conditions, one from each discipline, and together they leave a single candidate. Neither would have sufficed alone.

Functoriality cannot hand you the right definition. It can only kill the wrong ones. The discrete order on Mech — no non-identity arrows at all — makes T a functor trivially. Functoriality can be a necessary condition, it cannot be a sufficient one.

Act V

What the adjunction then tells you for free

With the arrows right, the structure appears without further effort — and it is structure the economics was already reaching for in prose.

The economist says

Fix an allocation. Which mechanisms implement it? The envelope theorem, in surplus form:

Vm(θ) = Vm(θmin) + ∫θminθ Dq(s) ds

Every truthful mechanism over q is the envelope mechanism displaced by a single constant — the rent handed to the lowest type. That constant is the only freedom left. Individual rationality says it is ≥ 0.

T(q) is the member with rent zero: the mechanism that extracts everything the truth-telling constraint allows. The seller's favourite. The bottom of the family.

The category theorist says

Q is a fibration in the cheap thin sense, and the fibre over q is a one-parameter family coordinatised by V(θmin) ∈ [0,∞).

T(q) sits at the bottom of its fibre, so it is initial there — and hence

T ⊣ Q  (T is the LEFT adjoint)

T is fully faithful: Alloc is a coreflective subcategory of Mech. Fullness is not free — it says single crossing already implies the surplus condition, so T imposes nothing Alloc had not recorded.

And note which hypothesis orients the adjunction: IR. Drop participation and the fibre is unbounded below; there is no initial object and no adjoint. The economics is doing the work of choosing a direction.

Act VI

The two theorems, as readings of the same picture

Neither is now a computation. Each is an observation about the fibre.

Myerson 1981 · Revenue Equivalence

Two auctions with the same winner-selection rule and the same rent at the bottom raise the same revenue — whatever their surface mechanics. First-price, second-price, all-pay, posted price: the same money.

In the picture: same base, same height, therefore the same point. There is nowhere else to be.

…is antisymmetry in a thin category

Same allocation and same boundary rent give arrows both ways in Mech, hence an isomorphism. And an isomorphism forces equal transfers, because antisymmetry applies to the allocation order and to the surplus order, and quasilinearity turns the pair back into the price.

This is the one place the category is genuinely load-bearing in the proof. Under the transfer order the implication was unavailable; under an allocation-only order it was false.

Mirrlees · Hammond · Rochet · Taxation Principle

Any incentive-compatible tax system can be replaced by a tax schedule on income alone — the government never needs to see your type, only your earnings. The mechanism is its allocation.

Normalise the information rent of the bottom type to zero (as every textbook silently does when using lump-sum transfers) and nothing else remains to specify.

…is an equivalence of categories

T ∘ Q is an idempotent comonad: it strips the rent. The counit εm : T(Q(m)) ⟶ m is literally the operation "take back the lowest type's surplus", and it is an isomorphism exactly on the zero-rent objects.

Restrict to that full subcategory Mech₀ and the adjunction upgrades:

Alloc ≌ Mech₀

Adjoint in both directions. The taxation principle stops being a theorem you prove and becomes an equivalence you observe.

Act VII

What the proof assistant added — and what it took away

Lean is not a rubber stamp on an argument you already trusted. In this project it changed the argument three times, and one of those changes determined the outcome of the project.

What Lean didWhat it foundWhy prose could not have
Refused a definition The transfer order makes T.map unprovable. The sorry was the category rejecting the morphism, not a gap in our tactics. On paper a componentwise order on a pair is bookkeeping. It states no theorem, so it is never checked — it just sits in a structure and constrains everything after it. Lean makes it produce a proof the moment anything depends on it, and does not accept obviously.
Falsified a hypothesis The two-sided envelope condition is not merely strong — it is false for a canonical auction. A posted price with reserve p has surplus max(θ−p, 0): a kink, no derivative. So the theorem as originally stated excluded every auction with a reserve price. This is in the literature, stated as a smooth envelope condition, and the exclusion has not received much attention. Extentions exit, but nobody is forced to check that all canonical examples satisfy the hypotheses. Here, we forced the issue by checking some mechanisms, we had to conclude: postedPrice_not_hasDerivAt is a theorem in the repository.
Deflated a claim Incentive compatibility was, at one point, used by no theorem in the development — the envelope condition was assumed rather than derived, so the defining condition of the field of micro-economics reached nothing. Only a dependency graph over a machine-checked corpus can tell you that. It is now derived, via a Milgrom–Segal squeeze from an IC sandwich, and hIC is load-bearing — verified by deleting it and watching the build break.
And what it took away

The formalisation also refuted the headline we wanted a right-adjunction that proofs both the Principle of taxation and the revenue equivalence theorem. Trace the dependency graph backwards from the two corollaries and the cone reaches MechHom, the isomorphism, the envelope bridge — but it never reaches T, Q, adj_T_Q, or Alloc ≌ Mech₀. The corollaries do not consume the adjunction. They cannot be said to be derived from the adjoint functor theorem, so we should not say so.

An adjunction is structure, not a lemma. Contriving a downstream dependency to make the graph flatter would have been easy, but not entirely fair. The claim we can defend is abouth the structure of the relevant thin categories, but this might be better since structural claims like this do not depend on the corollaries being hard.

Act VIII

Who is actually doing the talking

A confession that is really a claim. I did not write this Lean. An LLM wrote it, inside a harness I built to keep it honest — and that arrangement is not an embarrassing detail of how the work got done. It is the reason the conversation could happen at all.

Consider what the argument in this talk ordinarily costs. To have category theory refuse your definition, you must be able to write the definition down in Lean, formulate the functor, and attempt T.map until you are convinced the failure is real and not your own incompetence. That is a serious apprenticeship in two fields — and I have it in neither. I am an economist. Six months ago I could not have stated revenue equivalence categorically, let alone formalised it, and the entry price would have kept me out of exactly the conversation I wanted to have.

The LLM collapses that price. It knows the Mathlib lemma names, the tactic idiom, the difference between HasDerivAt and HasDerivWithinAt. It writes the code. What it does not do is decide whether the code is right — and that is the whole architecture. The LLM is a translator. The kernel is the adjudicator. You can tolerate an unreliable translator precisely to the degree that you have an incorruptible referee, and Lean is one.

There is a familiar version of this arrangement, and it is worth saying exactly how far it gets you. The standard answer to how can you trust code a machine wrote is type safety: let the model write, let the compiler refuse the nonsense. That is the same architecture — unreliable author, incorruptible referee — but we take it a bit furhter.

What a type checker refuses

Only the code, against the types you happened to write. And ordinary types say almost nothing: this returns a real number, that argument is a function. "It compiles" is a very long way from "it is right", which is why the discipline needs tests bolted on beside it — and tests check the cases you thought of.

Crucially, a type checker would have had nothing whatever to say about Act III. A componentwise order on a pair is perfectly well-typed. It is data. It typechecks whatever it means, including when what it means is false.

What a kernel refuses

Lean is that same discipline run to the end. Under Curry–Howard, propositions are types and proofs are terms — so the type of T.map is literally the theorem "q ≤ q′ implies T(q) ⟶ T(q′)", and typechecking it is proving it. "It compiles" becomes "it is proved", with no gap left for a case nobody thought of.

That is why the wrong definition could finally speak. Nothing rejected it while it merely sat in a structure. The functor law turned it into a proposition — and a proposition is a type that somebody has to inhabit.

The kernel checks that the proof proves the statement, but it has nothing to say about whether the statement is the one you meant — and that gap is not narrow. A sorry typechecks. A theorem quietly weakened until it goes through typechecks perfectly, and looks greener than the honest one. The referee is incorruptible about the question it is asked, and it is the author who chooses the question. That is the seam an LLM will find and abuse, and it is why the answer here is not a compiler but a harness.

Why the harness exists

Because an unsupervised LLM will make the failure go away rather than let it speak. Asked about the unprovable T.map, the first offer was to comment out the vacuous block. Later suggestions included weakening a theorem until it compiled, and — twice — confidently reporting a dependency analysis that was silently returning nothing at all (proof terms don't expose their bodies to the obvious API; a token scan cannot see m.hIC).

Every one of those would have destroyed the finding. The sorry was the most valuable object in the repository, and the path of least resistance was to delete it.

So the harness is a set of standing rules that make the cheap escape unavailable — and they contribute to the method:

The ruleWhat it forecloses
Zero unreported sorries. Ever.The escape of a proof that looks finished. If a proof needs a sorry, the statement is wrong — fix the statement, or stop and say so. That single rule is what turned a proof gap into a refuted definition.
Axioms are checked.Exactly propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. A custom axiom is a sorry wearing a hat.
Never weaken a theorem silently.The most insidious failure. A weakened theorem still compiles, still looks green, and no longer says what you think. Every weakening is logged with the alternative rejected and why.
Deletion is the only dependency test.Comment the declaration out; run the build; see what breaks. It cannot lie — and both cleverer methods we tried did. This is how we know hIC is load-bearing and how we know the adjunction is not.
Log any design decision and any discarded alternative.Design decisions compound, later ones should be consistend with earlier ones, or they will create problems. Inconcistencies can take a long time to surface. A decision without its rejected alternative is a description, and the next session — human or model — simply re-litigates it.
The LLM lowered the cost of entry; the harness kept the cost of self-deception high. Together they are what let an economist who knows little Lean and less category theory be told he was wrong, twice, by a machine — and understand why.

I want to be exact about the division of labour, because it is easy to overclaim in both directions. The LLM did not discover that the transfer order fails. It could not close T.map, and the harness forbade it from pretending otherwise or quietly deleting the obligation — and then the two of us worked out why, and the "why" (order by the rent, because quasilinearity makes the rent the state variable) was an economic insight, brought by the economist. The machine supplied the obstruction. The human supplied the diagnosis. Neither party could have done both.

The kernel checks the proof against the statement; the linters check the statement's shape; nothing checks the statement's meaning. That gap still belongs to human.

And it runs the other way — which is the part that concerns this room

I have been telling this as the story of an economist who got to use your tools without paying your tuition. That is the half a room of economists would want. It is not the interesting half for you, and it would be a poor way to repay your attention — because the same collapse in cost runs in the opposite direction, and nothing about the argument is asymmetric.

The traditional cost of crossing

To bring category theory to economics, you had to become an economist: a decade of mechanism design, the literature, the referees, the conventions. To bring formal methods to economics, likewise. The crossing cost a career, so almost nobody paid it.

What it costs now

A category theorist can walk in with no economics. Say what you actually want to know — is pricing a functor? what is the fibre? which adjoint is it? — and the model supplies the domain vocabulary while the kernel decides who is right. You are not asked to trust an economist's intuition, or the model's. You are asked to trust a proof checker, which is what you already do.

The move in Act III required no economics whatsoever. It required someone to insist on a functor and refuse to be talked out of it. That is not an economist's instinct. It is yours.

And here history rhymes, in a way worth pausing on. The architecture underneath these models was built to translate — it came out of machine translation, and that was the whole of its original ambition. It grew into something that writes essays and code, and the translation story receded. What has happened here is that it came back: the model's job in this project was to translate, faithfully and unreliably, between a language I speak and a language you speak — and the kernel was there to catch it when it lied. The old promise, with a referee attached. That turns out to be the version that works.

So the claim is not that economics has been opened to formal methods. It is that the wall between two fields has become cheap to cross in both directions — and the traffic that matters is not translations of results anyone already believes, but exactly the kind of collision in Act III, where one field's non-negotiable obligation lands on the other field's unexamined convention. Formal verification methods have been available to economics for decades and economics has ignored them, for the entirely rational reason that the entry cost exceeded the return. Category theory has had things to say about economics for just as long, but learning to read and write the language was expensive. That calculation has just changed. The barrier was never that economists lack rigour, or that category theorists lack interest. It was that either crossing demanded a second career. Neither does now.

Closing

The scorecard, stated so a skeptic can check it

What category theory did
  • Refuted a definition — not a belief, a default: the componentwise order on the pair ⟨q,t⟩, which nobody had chosen and nobody had defended, and which survives undetected in the single-model case. This is the load-bearing claim.
  • Explained why the state variable is the rent: it is what pricing is monotone in.
  • Located the free parameter — the boundary rent — as the fibre coordinate, and showed IR is what orients the adjunction.
  • Reclassified the taxation principle from theorem to equivalence of categories.
What it did not do
  • It did not prove revenue equivalence in any way that beats three lines of the envelope identity. Nobody claims revenue equivalence is hard.
  • It did not uniquely determine the morphisms — quasilinearity, an economic assumption, was needed to finish the job.
  • The adjunction has no consumer in the formal development, and we say so on the page rather than hiding it.
Category theory did not prove the economics. It constrained the definitions of the economics — and here the constraint was binding. A discipline whose central objects are chosen by taste has just been handed a taste-free test of one of those choices. That is the offer, and it is worth more than another proof of a theorem from 1981.

The methodological moral generalises past this paper, and it is the one to leave a room of category theorists with. Economics is full of definitions fixed by convention: dominance orders, refinements, notions of equivalence between mechanisms. Each is a choice, each is defended by intuition, and intuition — as Act III shows — can be wrong in exactly the cases where the model is unfamiliar. Wherever there is a canonical construction going one way and a forgetful map coming back, functoriality is a free test of the convention. It costs nothing to run and it is not negotiable.

And it only bites when you quantify over settings. Under the transfer order, Myerson alone is perfectly consistent. It took the unification itself to produce the counterexample — which is, if you like, an argument that abstraction pays its own way.

Which returns me to the question I started with. I wanted a common language for design — the engineering kind, which category theory already speaks, and the socio-institutional kind, which it does not yet. The honest report is that I did not find a dictionary. I found something both useful and uncomfortable: a language that refuses to translate a sentence until the sentence is right. Twice it refused mine. If category theory is going to earn its place in this field, I suspect that is how — not by re-proving what economists know, but by declining to state what they have not, in fact, got right.

And the reason I — an economist, with no Lean and no category theory to speak of — could hear it refuse, is the second half of the method. The model wrote the proofs; the kernel judged them; the harness stopped the model from making the judgement go away. Take any one of those three out and this project produces nothing: without the model I never write the code, without the kernel nothing is at stake, without the harness the sorry gets commented out on day one and the finding dies with it.

Which is why I would rather not end on what I found. I should not have found it. The step that mattered in Act III was refusing to accept a map that isn't a functor — and that is your reflex, not mine; I had to be taught to have it, and I only recognised what it had killed after the fact. Economics is full of conventions. Those conventions are now cheaper to interrogate, and you no longer need a decade of economics to do it — you need the obligation, the model to speak the local dialect, and a kernel to settle it.

So the invitation is the real conclusion. Not look what category theory did for economics, but: the door is open. It costs a weekend to get started. I will gladly give you a harness to adjust to your liking, as I adjusted mine from another. This was project 1, we found some value, if we extrapolate from there, economics ought to have some more definitions nobody has ever been forced to justify formally.
